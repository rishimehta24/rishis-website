The story so far

  1. GTA

    Grew up in the Toronto area. Started volunteering in retirement homes with Ayaan when we were young. Spending time around older people was just part of life, long before it was a business.

  2. 2019 →

    Got into machine learning through activity and object classification. Did consulting work for XPRIZE, Sidewalk Labs and Oxford Properties while still in school. Learned how to break a messy problem down and ship something.

  3. HS

    Finished high school. Didn't take the university route; put the time into building a company and understanding senior care instead.

  4. Fallyx

    Built a waist-worn fall-detection wearable: a six-axis sensor with an on-device ML model that caught falls in real time and told staff where the resident was. Filed a patent on the detection method. Backed by 186 Ventures and Ivan Yuen (cofounder of Wattpad).

  5. 1.5 yrs

    Moved into a retirement home in Scarborough with Ayaan. Not “embedded”. Lived there. Slept there, ate three meals a day with residents, worked from the building. CBC came by around the holidays.

    This is the part that changed everything. A resident's quality of life had less to do with any one device and much more to do with the system around them: staff time, funding, and whether anyone actually understood what was happening to each person.

  6. The gap

    Watched one fall get scattered across 7–10 separate notes. Watched reporting eat roughly a fifth of a nursing manager's week. The information was already being collected; nobody was connecting it.

  7. Ascenix

    Rebuilt the company around that. Ascenix plugs into the EHR homes already use, automates incident reporting, flags missing compliance documentation and ranks resident risk. Team of seven. Live in 55 facilities.

  8. Sep 2026

    Got into Y Combinator's Fall 2026 batch. Moved to San Francisco. Most new demand is coming from the US now, so that's where the focus is.

  9. Now

    Working on the infrastructure that runs senior care. Reporting was the first piece. Reimbursement, resident health and better care are the rest of it.

02

Ascenix

The incident intelligence layer for long-term care.

Care teams write everything down already: falls, behaviours, hydration, assessments, care plans. It's just fragmented across thousands of notes, and government mandates say it all has to be analysed anyway. Ascenix reads that documentation against itself and sends back the reporting, the compliance gaps and the ranked risk. Nothing gets written twice.

We're hiring engineers and account executives who want to work on something that matters to real people. If that's you, write to me directly.

How I operate

  1. Live up to it.

    The thing I actually worry about isn't failing. It's not using what I was given. Everything else is downstream of that.

  2. Go live there.

    You don't understand a problem from a deck or a discovery call. I understand senior care because I ate breakfast with residents for a year and a half.

  3. Money is a tool.

    I've never cared much about things. Money is useful because it makes things happen faster. That's the whole appeal.

  4. No retirement.

    Building a company is the most fulfilled version of me I've found. I don't picture stopping, and I don't want to.

  5. Say it plainly.

    Generic, over-polished or needlessly complicated answers get pushed back on. If it fits in one sentence, it gets one sentence.

  6. Discipline over mood.

    Less TV. Fewer distractions. Protect the focus and make the day count. Motivation is nice; it's not the plan.
