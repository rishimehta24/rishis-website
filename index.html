Rishi Mehta

Cofounder & CEO of Ascenix (YC F26). From Toronto, living in San Francisco.

I build software for senior care. Ascenix takes the clinical notes care teams already write and turns them into incident reports, compliance checks and a clear picture of resident risk. It's live in 55 facilities across Canada and the US, and we're a team of seven.

I got here by moving into a retirement home straight out of high school. Ayaan Esmail (my cofounder) and I lived there for about a year and a half: slept there, ate with the residents, worked from the building. That's where most of what I know about this industry comes from.

The story so far

  • Grew up in Toronto. Started volunteering in retirement homes with Ayaan when we were young, so older people were part of life long before they were a business.
  • Got into machine learning in 2019 through activity and object classification. Did consulting work for XPRIZE, Sidewalk Labs and Oxford Properties while still in school.
  • Finished high school and didn't take the university route. Put the time into building a company and understanding senior care instead.
  • Built Fallyx: a waist-worn fall-detection wearable with a six-axis sensor and an on-device ML model. Filed a patent on the detection method. Backed by 186 Ventures and Ivan Yuen, cofounder of Wattpad.
  • Moved into a retirement home in Scarborough with Ayaan to understand the problem properly. CBC came by around the holidays.
  • Learned that a resident's quality of life had less to do with any single device and more to do with the system around them: staff time, funding, and whether anyone understood what was happening to each person.
  • Watched one fall get scattered across 7–10 separate notes, and reporting eat roughly a fifth of a nursing manager's week. The information was already being collected. Nobody was connecting it.
  • Rebuilt the company around that as Ascenix. Live in 55 facilities, team of seven.
  • Got into Y Combinator's Fall 2026 batch and moved to San Francisco.
  • Now working on the infrastructure that runs senior care. Reporting was the first piece; reimbursement, resident health and better care are the rest of it.

Ascenix

The incident intelligence layer for long-term care. Care teams already document everything: falls, behaviours, hydration, assessments, care plans. It's fragmented across thousands of notes, and government mandates say it all has to be analysed anyway. Ascenix plugs into the EHR homes already use, reads that documentation, and sends back the reporting, the compliance gaps and the ranked risk. Nothing gets written twice.

  • 55 facilities across Canada and the US
  • About 20% of a nursing manager's week given back from manual reporting
  • 7–10 notes per fall turned into one report

We're hiring engineers and account executives. If you want to work on something that matters to real people, email me directly. More at ascenix.co.

How I operate

  • Live up to it. The thing I actually worry about isn't failing. It's not using what I was given.
  • Go live there. You don't understand a problem from a deck or a discovery call. I understand senior care because I ate breakfast with residents for a year and a half.
  • Money is a tool. I've never cared much about things. Money is useful because it makes things happen faster.
  • No retirement. Building a company is the most fulfilled version of me I've found. I don't picture stopping.
  • Say it plainly. Generic, over-polished or needlessly complicated answers get pushed back on. If it fits in one sentence, it gets one.
  • Discipline over mood. Less TV, fewer distractions, protect the focus. Motivation is nice; it isn't the plan.
