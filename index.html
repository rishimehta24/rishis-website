Why senior care

I believe in a better tomorrow for the elderly, and I believe the status quo isn't good enough. Everyone gets old. Most of us will end up in a care home or have a parent in one, and today those places run on overworked staff, paper, and luck.

Living in one is what made it personal. You eat with people every day, learn their names and their stories, then watch a fall go unnoticed or a report eat an afternoon that should have gone to them. I kept trying ideas: a watch, a sensor, a wearable, a patent. Most failed. Ascenix is the one that worked, and it's still just the first piece.

Ascenix

Care teams already document everything. It's fragmented across thousands of notes, and government mandates say it all has to be analysed anyway. Ascenix plugs into the EHR homes already use and sends back the reporting, the compliance gaps and the ranked resident risk. Nothing gets written twice.

  • 55 facilities across Canada and the US
  • About 20% of a nursing manager's week given back
  • 7–10 notes per fall turned into one report
  • Hiring engineers and AEs. Email me, or see ascenix.co.

How I operate

  • Live up to it. The thing I worry about isn't failing. It's not using what I was given.
  • Go live there. You don't understand a problem from a deck. I ate breakfast with residents for a year and a half.
  • Money is a tool. I've never cared much about things. Money makes things happen faster.
  • No retirement. Building is the most fulfilled version of me I've found. I don't picture stopping.
  • Say it plainly. If it fits in one sentence, it gets one.
  • Discipline over mood. Motivation is nice; it isn't the plan.
